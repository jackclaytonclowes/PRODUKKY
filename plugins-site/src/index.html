Fracture Crate Install Beta

Two plugins.
One makes things break.
One makes drums dusty.

FRACTURE splits your sound into bands and drives each one its own way. CRATE makes drums sound like they came off a late-1980s sampler. Both are audio effects for your DAW, and both are in beta.

Formats
Audio Unit, VST3, standalone app
System
macOS 10.13 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel
Hosts
Logic, GarageBand, Live, Reaper, Bitwig, Cubase
Network
None. The plugins never connect to anything

01  Fracture v{{version:fracture}}

FRACTURE

Multi-band multi-FX distortion

Split the sound into up to three bands and distort each differently, so the top can crush while the bass stays clean. Around that: a bit crusher, a feedback loop that can ring at a musical note, a filter that moves in time with your song, and a modulation matrix to animate any of it.

  • Fifteen drive modes, from tube and tape warmth to wave folding, wrapping and bit destruction, in two stages per band, all anti-aliased where it matters
  • Table mode: draw the harmonics yourself, like a wavetable, in four frames, and sweep between them with an LFO for a wobbling harmonic movement
  • Feedback that repeats in milliseconds, rings at a note in tune to a cent, or echoes on the beat, and can go back through the drive for growls
  • A filter with clean, analogue and vintage circuits up to 48 dB, whose curve you can see and drag, and a rhythm that moves it in time
  • Two LFOs, an envelope follower, a six-slot matrix, an XY pad and two macros
  • Each band can drive the whole stereo image, only its centre, or only its width
  • 49 presets, levelled so switching compares sounds rather than volumes, with arrows to step through them
  • Undo, A/B compare, and presets of your own, saved as plain files

02  Crate v{{version:crate}}

CRATE

Twelve-bit drum processor

The gritty, dusty, slightly swung sound of boom-bap records made on an SP-1200 or an Akai S900. Put it on a drum bus or a loop. It is an effect, not a sampler, so your drums, sequencer and tempo stay as they are.

  • Two machines: the SP, bright and aliased, and the S900, dark and smooth behind steep filters
  • The pitch trick: the old move of sampling a record sped up and slowing it back down, for the SP's particular grain
  • Twelve-bit converter with clock, bits, companding and anti-alias you can hear working, a meter showing how many of its bits you are using, and Keep sub to leave the kick's low end clean
  • A four-pole filter that each drum hit opens and lets close, like the SP's first two outputs, plus a rhythm that moves it in time
  • Dust, swing and push that follow your DAW's grid
  • 27 presets with arrows to step through them, undo, A/B compare, and presets of your own

03  Installing on a Mac

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg.
  2. Double-click the installer in it: Install both plugins.command (in a single-plugin image, Install FRACTURE or Install CRATE).
  3. macOS will probably refuse the first time and say it is from an unidentified developer. That is expected: these builds are not signed with an Apple developer account, so Apple has nothing to check them against. Right-click the file, choose Open, then Open again in the dialog.
  4. Terminal opens, copies the plugins into your own plug-in folders and runs Apple's Audio Unit validator. If it says PASSED, you are done.
  5. Restart your DAW. In Logic, look under Audio Units, then Fracture or Crate.

Drag the app to Applications if you want the standalone version too.

Use the installer, do not drag the plugins in yourself. A dragged copy keeps the mark macOS puts on every download, and Logic then refuses it: "could not be opened", or a failed validation. The installer clears that mark and re-signs each plugin where it lands.

If Logic says it could not be opened

Usually the plugins were dragged in rather than installed. Quit Logic, open Terminal, run these two lines, then start Logic again:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/*.component ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/*.vst3
killall -9 AudioComponentRegistrar

In Logic, Settings, then Plug-in Manager, choose Reset & Rescan Selection for each.

If it does not show up in Logic

Quit Logic, open Terminal, run this, then start Logic again:

killall -9 AudioComponentRegistrar

What it installs, and where

~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/FRACTURE.component
~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/FRACTURE.vst3

(and the same two for CRATE). Nothing else is touched: no background processes, no login items, no network access. To uninstall, delete those files.

04  Where it is: beta

Tested by machine, not yet by ear. Save your project before loading either plugin on something you care about, and keep Safety clip on (it is, by default).

What has been checked: both pass pluginval at its strictest level, and several hundred measurements run on every change. They cover the drive curves matching their reference to twelve decimal places, filters landing on their marked frequencies, tuned feedback within a cent of its note, reported latency matching the real latency, and no zipper noise when knobs move. Also checked: a bounce comes out the same every time, and every preset stays near the dry level without clipping.

What has not: nobody has sat with them in a real session yet. That is what you are being asked to do.

What would be useful to hear back

  • Does anything crackle, zipper or click when you move a knob quickly?
  • Does a bounce sound the same as playback?
  • Does the panel make sense without anyone explaining it?
  • Which presets do you keep, and which would you cut?
  • Which DAW and Mac you tried it on, and whether it loaded
