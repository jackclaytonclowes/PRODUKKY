# STOOP — how it works

STOOP puts a loop through the kind of effects the first pocket samplers of the mid-2000s had, the ones a whole generation of beats was resampled through: a pitch shifter, a filter with drive, a lo-fi crusher, and above all the **vinyl simulator** with the knob the later model dropped, **Comp**. Put it on a loop, a sample, a drum bus or a whole beat. It is an effect, not a sampler: your sounds, your sequencer and your tempo stay as they are.

The sound goes through the panel in order: **Pitch**, then **Filter + drive**, then **Lo-fi**, then the **Vinyl sim**, which comes last so the record's compression and wobble sit over everything. Each section has its own **On** switch, like the hardware's effect buttons. Switching one on or off fades over a hundredth of a second, so it never clicks.

## Quick start

- Straight away you hear the vinyl sim: a little compression, a little crackle, a little wobble. That is **Init**.
- Open the preset menu at the top right and try **Vinyl sim, the original**, **Comp only** and **Slowed and dusty**. Between them they cover most of what STOOP does.
- The preset menu groups the factory presets under headings. The **arrows** either side of it step to the previous and next preset without opening the list.
- **Favourites**: choose **Add this preset to favourites** at the bottom of the menu and it is listed at the top from then on. Choose it again (it now says Remove) to take it off.
- Presets are levelled, so switching compares sounds, not volumes.
- Turn **Mix** down to blend the effect with the clean sound. If it gets too loud, turn **Output** down. **Safety clip** stops anything going over 0 dB.

## Faded controls and tooltips

- A faded control does nothing with the current settings. Hover over it and the tooltip says why, for example "Pitch is switched off". You can still move it, ready for later.
- **Tips** at the top turns these hover notes on and off. The choice is remembered.
- **Guide** opens this page. Press it again to close it.
- **Knobs**: drag up or down. Double-click puts one back to its default. **Click the number under a knob to type a value**, in the units it shows (1.2k, 800, -6, 50%), then press Enter; Escape leaves it as it was.

## Undo, compare and your presets

- **Undo** and **Redo** at the top take back and redo changes: each knob drag, each preset you pick. Ctrl + Z and Ctrl + Shift + Z do the same (Cmd on a Mac), when the panel has the keyboard.
- **A** and **B** are two settings to flip between, so you can compare an idea with what you had. B starts as a copy of A. **A to B** copies what you hear now into the other one. Each keeps its own undo, and both are saved with your song.
- **Save**, next to the preset menu, saves your settings as a preset of your own. They appear under **Your presets** in the menu, and **Show the presets folder** opens the folder, in Documents/STOOP/Presets.

## 01 Input

- **Input** sets how hard you hit the rest of the box. The compressor and the drive both respond to it.
- **Mono** sums left and right before anything else.

## 02 Pitch

- **Pitch** shifts the sound up or down, up to two octaves either way, without changing the tempo. It defaults to an octave down.
- **Feedback** sends the shifted sound back through the shifter, so it shifts again, and again, each time quieter: a fifth up climbs in fifths. At 0 there is only the one shift.
- **Balance** mixes the shifted sound with the original. At 100% you hear only the shifted sound; at 50%, both.
- This is the old-fashioned kind of pitch shifter, and it sounds like one: a slight warble, and a little doubling, as it splices the sound together every few hundredths of a second.

## 03 Filter + drive

- **Type** is low pass (takes the top off), band pass (keeps a band) or high pass (takes the bottom off).
- **Cutoff** is where the filter acts, and **Resonance** makes it ring there.
- **Drive** overdrives the sound after the filter, so a resonant peak is what breaks up first. At 0 it is clean. Turning it up makes it dirtier and a little louder, but not as much louder as it is dirtier.

## 04 Vinyl sim

The heart of the box. The original sampler's vinyl simulator had three knobs: compressor, noise and wow flutter. Its successor kept noise and wow flutter, put a tone control where the compressor had been, and the people who own both say the original sounds softer and less tinny. **Comp** is that knob, back.

- **Comp** is the compression a record is cut with. One knob does it all: further round, it starts compressing at a lower level and compresses harder. It turns the quiet parts up to meet the loud ones, so the overall level stays about the same while the sound gets denser and more even. The picture under it shows the curve it is using and what it is doing right now; the red bar is how much it is turning down.
- **Noise** is the record's surface: hiss, crackle, a low rumble, and a scratch that ticks once every time the record goes round. It is added after the compression, the way it really happens, so the crackle never pumps with the music.
- **Wow flutter** is the turntable: a slow wobble once a revolution, as if the record were not quite centred, a slower drift, and a fast flutter on top. At full the pitch swings about 1% either way.
- **Speed** is 33, 45 or 78: how often the wobble comes round, and the scratch with it.
- The record on the panel turns at that speed, sits off centre by as much as the wobble, and shows the scratch in red. Underneath, it says how far off speed it is playing right now.

## 05 Lo-fi

- **Rate** lowers the sample rate, with nothing to smooth it, so high sounds fold back as metallic grit.
- **Bits** lowers the resolution. Fewer bits is noisier and rougher; each bit you take away adds about 6 dB of noise.

## 06 Out

- **Mix** blends the whole effect with the clean sound, and **Output** is the final level.
- The meters show the level coming in and going out.

## Good to know

- STOOP holds the sound back about 9 milliseconds (the wobble needs room to move either way). Your DAW makes up for it automatically, and the delay never changes, whatever you switch on.
- Nothing in STOOP is random from one playback to the next: the same bar always crackles and wobbles the same way when you play it from the same place.
- Nobody has compared STOOP with the original hardware yet. Every number on the panel is measured; whether it sounds like the real thing is for your ears.
